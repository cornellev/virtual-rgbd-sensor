As a perception lead and autonomy engineer on Cornell Electric Vehicles, I have contributed significantly to the perception stack for out autonomous vehicles, including having built a Rust LiDAR driver with a calibrated sensor measurement model, decoding raw UDP packets and applying range, angular, timing, and sensor-offset corrections to generate 3D point clouds for live obstacle detection. Developed C++ and Rust implementations of real-time two-layer-graph clustering on 80k+ points, reducing LiDAR scan latency 10× to 20–30 ms for dynamic obstacle region generation and detection, tracking, and longer-horizon planning. Implemented a real-time point cloud to occupancy grid algorithm that constructs accurate representations of known free-space and obstacle regions around the vehicle, enabling accurate and continuous spatial mapping for path planning. Integrated camera–LiDAR fusion to combine RGB imagery with precise depth measurements for downstream algorithms. 
Implemented LiDAR-perception algorithms like point cloud segmentation, obstacle tracking, bounding box and occupancy grid generation in C++. Built simple CPU-process simulator and minishell in C.
